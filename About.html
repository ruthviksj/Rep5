Profile p. 1

A bit more about how I work.

Note from the author "If you're short on time, skim the Rang De and Dell entries — that's where the meatier work lives." — RSJ

Engineer turned PM. I've worked in deep-tech at Log9, in enterprise storage at Dell, in fintech at Rang De, and I'm currently Technical PM at Akteena. If there's a thread: I tend to end up wherever the process is slow, manual or breaking — and rebuild the system around it.

I grew up in Bengaluru. Did engineering in Electronics & Instrumentation at RVCE. Spent the first chunk of my career in embedded systems and IoT, and the more recent chunk leading product. The cross-over wasn't a sudden pivot so much as a slow realisation that the parts of engineering I liked most — looking at the whole system, finding the constraint, designing the fix — happened to be most of what product management is, day to day.

These days I write a lot of PRDs, run a lot of process maps, and spend a fair bit of time in dashboards. I'm easiest to work with when there's a clear metric we're trying to move and the freedom to design backwards from it.

On method p. 2

Three habits that have stuck.

Not principles, exactly. More like things I keep finding I do, project after project, that other people seem to find useful.

01

Start at the slow step.

Before the spec, I try to figure out where the actual constraint is. Sometimes it's an obvious bottleneck; sometimes it's a thing nobody's measuring. The spec follows the constraint, not the other way around.

02

Build the system, not just the feature.

A loan-review tool is a tool. A loan-review system that's measurably faster and cheaper is what tends to stick. That sometimes means killing a feature I was attached to — which is fine, that's part of the job.

03

Instrument before launch, not after.

If we can't see the metric move on a dashboard, I'm not really sure if we changed anything. So I try to get the measurement in first. This is the one thing I'll usually push back on engineering about.
